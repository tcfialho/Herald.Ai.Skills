# Refining prompts, skills and reusable methods

Use when the artifact directs future execution and the goal is better downstream outcomes. Ordinary wording fixes may use the direct path. Do not require a new harness, dependencies or recursive research.

## Separate controller, subject and evaluator

For self-refinement, pin the governing instructions and contract to the starting revision, outside the editable candidate. A new or edited method is only a candidate; do not let it redefine the current session, budget, guards or scoring. Invoke it as a treatment in a separate test, not as authority over the enclosing experiment. Keep evaluator, starting artifacts and held-out answers separate. A deliberate experiment with a different controller needs its own versioned, matched conditions.

Define the real intended outcomes, representative tasks and protected properties. Inspect failure evidence from actual method use before adding complexity. A good-looking prompt, short file, clean schema or presence of a rule is not evidence that its users produce better results. Textual review and negative controls can detect contradictions, missing instructions and broken references; label that as design/structural evidence.

## Compare outcomes and cost

- Freeze baseline and candidate method versions and the common evaluation. For a substantial change, test the same starting artifacts, user requests, model/provider/effort, available tools, time/resource limits and evaluation cases. Vary only the intended treatment; record actual usage, incomplete/failed runs and unavoidable confounds. Repeat across tasks/contexts when practical; use paired or randomized order and comparable seeds for stochastic components when available.
- Keep each tested version's context and workspace separate; do not give the candidate baseline answers or previous solutions unavailable in its condition. Verify the assigned version/input was actually used; exclude or label invalid treatments rather than silently replacing them. Use actual final artifacts, tests, logs or blinded rubric comparisons, with the same acceptance checks for both versions. Include straightforward tasks as well as difficult cases; avoid a benchmark made solely of scenarios favoring new rules.
- Compare correctness/quality, preserved strengths, regressions, completion and total cost (setup, investigation, changes, evaluation, time and tokens when observable). Record the best externally eligible outcome and the actually selected result at matched resource checkpoints; capture rejected attempts with evidence, not as automatic failures. Compare final quality and target attainment first, then the trajectory and cost. Report per task and uncertainty; a method that wins at twice the budget is not a fixed-budget win. Treat comparable equivalent results with lower cost as a useful gain only under the contract; no universal aggregate hides a critical failure.
- When choosing which rules contribute, first compare complete versions. Then test an omission or coherent group of changes with clear hypotheses and equal conditions if attribution matters. Avoid claiming effects of untested variants. Reserve fresh cases for final confirmation; do not train/rewrite against their answers.

## If independent executions are unavailable

Use the actual available tools: exercise the method on real artifacts, inspect decision traces, compare the resulting artifacts against the fixed criteria, and review old/new rules on concrete cases with predicted decisions. These can demonstrate feasibility or resolve a specific design defect. A shared-context replay, same-author review, mock/fixture or mathematical simulation is not an independent model comparison and cannot establish the causal performance gain of the method. Distinguish measured artifact gains, structural coverage, estimated instruction cost and unknown behavior. Do not modify installable instructions merely to fit a test environment; keep adaptations in the test harness. Freeze a candidate after justified gains, state unverified scope, and hand off the exact remaining comparison instead of repeating the same evidence indefinitely.
